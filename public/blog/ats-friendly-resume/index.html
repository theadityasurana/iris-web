How to write an ATS-friendly resume that applicant tracking systems can read

Team Iris·November 2, 2026·3 min read
In shortAn ATS-friendly resume uses a single-column layout, standard section headings (Experience, Education, Skills), the same order for every role (title, company, dates), no important text in images, headers or footers, keywords that match the job description where they're true, and a text-based PDF or DOCX file.

At most companies, your resume is read by software before it's read by a person. Applicant tracking systems (ATS) pull out your name, contact details, job titles, dates and skills, and that extracted version is often what a recruiter searches and filters on.

A beautiful resume that the software can't parse is, in practice, a blank one. The good news: making your resume machine-readable takes about an hour, and it makes it easier for humans to read too.

What is an applicant tracking system (ATS)?

An applicant tracking system is software that companies use to collect, parse and filter job applications. It turns your resume into structured data (name, roles, dates, skills) so recruiters can search and sort candidates. If the parsing goes wrong, your experience may never show up in their searches.

What resume layout works best for ATS?

  • One column. Two-column layouts are often read left-to-right across both columns, scrambling your experience.
  • No text inside images, icons or charts. Skill bars and logos look nice but contain nothing a parser can read.
  • Keep important details out of headers and footers. Some systems skip them, so don't put your email or phone number only there.
  • Use standard fonts at a normal size. Decorative fonts can turn into garbled characters.

Which section headings should you use?

Parsers look for familiar labels. Stick to Experience, Education, Skills and Projects. A clever heading like "Where I've made an impact" might read well, but the software may not recognise it as your work history.

How should you format each job?

Keep the same order for every position:

  1. Job title
  2. Company name
  3. Location (optional)
  4. Start and end dates, in a consistent format like Jan 2024 – Mar 2026

Then add three to five bullet points that start with a verb and, where you can, a result: "Reduced report generation time from two days to two hours by automating the data pipeline."

How do you add the right keywords?

If the job asks for "stakeholder management" and your resume says "worked with cross-functional partners", a person sees the overlap but a keyword search might not. Where it's honestly true of your experience, use the same terms the job description uses. Don't add keywords you can't back up in an interview.

Should you send a PDF or a Word document?

A text-based PDF or DOCX works almost everywhere. Make sure it's a real text document, not a scan: if you can't select and copy the text, neither can the software.

How can you check your resume is parsed correctly?

When you upload your resume to Iris, we parse it the way an ATS would and show the result in your profile. If something comes out wrong, like a missing job title or jumbled dates, other systems are probably reading it wrong too. Fix it once, and every application you send from Iris carries the corrected version.

Frequently asked questions

Can an ATS read a two-column resume?

Often not reliably. Many systems read across both columns line by line, which mixes up your sections. A single-column layout is the safest choice.

Is a PDF or DOCX better for ATS?

Both work in most systems, as long as the file contains real, selectable text rather than a scanned image.

Should I copy keywords from the job description?

Use the job description's wording for skills and experience you genuinely have. Adding keywords you can't support usually backfires in the interview.

Do recruiters still read resumes themselves?

Yes, but usually after the ATS has parsed and filtered them. A clean, parseable resume makes sure you reach that stage.
