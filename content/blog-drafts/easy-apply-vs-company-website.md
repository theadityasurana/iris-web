You find a role, and there's a one-click button right there. Should you use it, or spend ten minutes on the company's own form?

## What Easy Apply is good at

Easy Apply and similar features let you send your profile and resume in seconds. For a job that fits, with few screening questions, that's a real benefit. You can apply to more roles in the time you have.

## Where it falls short

- **More competition.** Lower effort generally means more applicants, so yours is one of a larger pile.
- **Less context.** A short form gives you few chances to show why you fit.
- **Tracking.** You may hear less about what happened afterward, depending on how the employer has set things up.

## What the company site offers

Applying directly puts your application into the employer's own system, with the employer's full questions answered. You also get to confirm the role is real and current on the company's own site, which helps you [avoid fake job postings](/blog/how-to-spot-job-scams/).

The trade-off is time. Forms repeat what your resume says, and some systems make you create an account first.

## A simple rule

- **Roles you really want:** apply on the company site, and look for a referral.
- **Good fit, tight on time:** Easy Apply is fine.
- **Weak fit:** skip it either way.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do recruiters ignore Easy Apply applications?

Not as a rule. Many recruiters review them, though practices vary by company. Fit and a clear resume still matter most.

### Can I do both for the same job?

Avoid it. Two applications for one role can create duplicate records. Choose one route.
