Once you pass about ten applications, you can't keep them in your head. A role you can't remember is a recruiter call you aren't ready for.

## The fields that matter

Most trackers fail because they ask for too much. These seven fields are enough:

1. **Company**
2. **Role**
3. **Link to the posting**
4. **Date applied**
5. **Status:** applied, in conversation, interview, offer, rejected, or closed
6. **Contact:** who you've spoken to, if anyone
7. **Next step and date**

Add a copy of the job description too. Postings get taken down, often just when you need one to prepare for an interview.

## Avoid applying twice

Search your tracker before you apply. Companies often post the same role more than once, or across several sites, and double applications look careless. Sorting by company makes this quick.

## The weekly review

Spend 15 minutes each week:

- Follow up on anything that's been quiet for over a week, where you have a contact. See [how to follow up](/blog/follow-up-after-applying-for-a-job/).
- Close anything clearly dead, so your list stays honest.
- Calculate your reply rate and compare it with the previous few weeks.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should I keep job application records?

Keep them until your search ends, then a while longer. They're useful if a company contacts you months later, and for your next job search.

### Should I track rejections?

Yes. Patterns in rejections, by role type or company size, tell you where to focus next.
