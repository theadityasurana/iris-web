You'll often hear that the first applicants get all the attention. There's some logic to it, but it needs care.

## Why early can help

Many teams review applications on a rolling basis. Once they have a few strong candidates moving through interviews, later applicants may get less attention. So applying in the first few days of a posting is a sensible habit where you can manage it.

We don't quote a specific "apply within X hours" figure, because no number holds across companies, roles and recruiters. Some teams wait and review everything in one batch.

## Why it isn't everything

A careful application on day five is better than a sloppy one in hour one. And some postings stay open for a long time because a role is hard to fill, so age alone doesn't mean a job is gone.

## A practical routine

1. **Check new roles on a regular schedule.** A set daily or every-other-day habit beats random bursts.
2. **Sort by most recent** on every job site you use.
3. **Set alerts** for your main titles and locations.
4. **Check older posts directly on the company site** before spending time on them. See [how to see when a job was posted](/blog/how-to-see-when-a-job-was-posted/).

## Why speed tends to be hard

The bottleneck usually isn't noticing the job. It's the 15 minutes of forms. If applying only takes a moment, you can act on new roles when you see them. ## Frequently asked questions

### Is it worth applying to a job posted a month ago?

Often yes, if it's still open on the company's site and it fits well. Keep your effort in proportion.

### What day of the week is best to apply?

There's no reliable answer that holds across employers. Applying soon after a role appears matters more than the weekday.
