"Add metrics to your resume" is easy advice until you realize nobody gave you a dashboard. Most jobs still have numbers, though. You may just need to look for them differently.

## Six kinds of numbers

- **Scale:** customers served, tickets handled, size of the codebase
- **Frequency:** daily, weekly or per-release activity
- **Time:** how long something took, before and after
- **Money:** budget managed, revenue touched, cost cut
- **Quality:** errors, returns, satisfaction scores
- **People:** team size, people trained, stakeholders involved

## Rewrites

**Before:** Responsible for handling customer support.

**After:** Resolved about 40 customer tickets a week and wrote the help articles that answered the most common questions.

**Before:** Improved the reporting process.

**After:** Cut the monthly reporting process from two days to half a day by automating the data pull.

## When you only have estimates

Honest approximations are fine: "about," "roughly," or "more than." Keep your reasoning in mind, since an interviewer may ask how you got the figure.

## When there really are no numbers

Describe the change instead. "Replaced a shared spreadsheet with a tracked workflow, so requests stopped getting lost" is concrete and believable.

## Where to find them

Check old performance reviews, emails, project documents and your own calendar. Ask a former colleague what they remember. Do it while you still have access, ideally before you need a new job.

## Never fabricate

An invented "increased revenue by 300%" invites questions you can't answer. A modest true number is far stronger. The same applies to tools that rewrite your resume: check what they produce. See [honest tailoring](/blog/tailor-resume-without-lying/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Does every bullet need a number?

No. Aim for numbers where they add meaning, with a mix of quantified and descriptive bullets.

### Can I share numbers from my past employer?

Avoid confidential figures. Use percentages or approximate ranges if exact numbers are sensitive.
