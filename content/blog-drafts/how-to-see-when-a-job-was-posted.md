A job that says "posted today" may have been open for weeks. Reposts, syncs between sites and auto-renewals can all reset the date you see.

## Checks you can do in a few minutes

### 1. Find it on the company's careers page

The employer's own listing is the best source. If the role isn't there, be cautious: it may already be closed. Many career pages show a posting date, and some don't.

### 2. Read the page source

Many job pages include structured data for search engines. In your browser, view the page source and search for `datePosted`. It isn't proof, as employers can update it, but it can be a useful clue.

### 3. Search for an exact phrase

Copy a distinctive sentence from the description and search for it in quotes. Older copies on other sites can give you a sense of how long it has been around.

### 4. Look for clues in the text

Things like "starting Q1" or a year in the description can reveal an old post.

## Why old listings stay up

Not every old listing is suspicious. Some roles are hard to fill, some companies hire continuously, and some listings are simply forgotten. A listing's age tells you to check further, not to give up.

## What to do with the answer

- **Fresh and on the company site:** apply soon.
- **Old but still on the company site:** apply if it's a strong fit, and try to find a contact.
- **Not on the company site at all:** skip it, or contact the company first. Our guide to [spotting job scams](/blog/how-to-spot-job-scams/) explains why.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do fake or ghost job postings exist?

Some listings aren't tied to an active hire, for instance because a role was filled or paused. Checking the company site and looking for recent activity helps you avoid spending time on them.

### Is a reposted job a red flag?

Not necessarily. It may mean the first search failed or the role reopened. Try to find a contact and ask.
