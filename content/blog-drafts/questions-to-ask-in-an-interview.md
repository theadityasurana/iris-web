"Do you have any questions for us?" is the part of the interview that belongs to you. Use it to find out what you really need to know.

## About the role

- What would success look like in the first six months?
- What's the hardest part of this job right now?
- Why is this role open?
- What would I be working on in my first month?

## About the team

- How does the team decide what to work on?
- How do you handle disagreement?
- Who would I work with most closely?
- What has changed most about the team over the past year?

## About the company

- What are the biggest priorities for the coming year?
- How does this team's work connect to those priorities?

## By interviewer

- **Recruiter:** What are the next steps, and what's the timeline?
- **Hiring manager:** What would make you glad you hired this person?
- **Future peer:** What do you wish you'd known before you joined?
- **Senior leader:** What are you most concerned about at the moment?

## Questions to hold back

Anything easily found on the company site, and details on leave or perks in a first interview, which are better saved for the offer stage. These are fair questions, but timing matters.

## Listen for what isn't said

If a hiring manager can't say what success looks like, that tells you something too. An interview is a two-way decision.

## Close clearly

Finish with: "Is there anything about my background that makes you hesitate?" It takes some nerve, but it gives you a chance to address a concern on the spot. Then send a [short thank-you note](/blog/thank-you-email-after-interview/).

## Frequently asked questions

### How many questions should I ask?

Two or three in a short slot. Prepare about five, as some will be answered along the way.

### Is it okay to take notes?

Yes. It shows interest, and it helps you later on.
