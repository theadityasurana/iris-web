The first call with a recruiter isn't usually deep. Its purpose is to confirm that there's no obvious mismatch before others spend time on you. It's also easy to lose through poor preparation.

## What they're checking

- Does your background match what the hiring manager asked for?
- Why do you want this job?
- Can you communicate clearly?
- Do salary, location and timing line up?
- Is anything likely to block the process, like work authorization?

## Prepare in 15 minutes

1. **Re-read the job description.** Have it open during the call.
2. **Look up the company.** What does it do? What's its recent news?
3. **Prepare your one-minute intro.** See [tell me about yourself](/blog/tell-me-about-yourself-answer/).
4. **Have your reason ready.** One specific sentence on why this role, not a general compliment.
5. **Have your numbers ready.** [Salary range](/blog/how-to-answer-salary-expectations/), notice period and start date.

## Practical details

Choose somewhere quiet with good signal. Keep your resume in front of you. Answer unknown numbers politely during your job search, or set a voicemail that sounds professional.

## Questions to ask

- What are the stages, and how long does the process usually take?
- What's most important to the hiring manager?
- Is there a budgeted range for this role?
- What are the next steps after this call?

## Mistakes to avoid

- Not knowing which job this is, which is why [tracking](/blog/how-to-track-job-applications/) matters
- Long, rambling answers
- Criticising a current employer
- Going into a salary discussion unprepared

## Frequently asked questions

### How long is a recruiter screen?

Often 15 to 30 minutes, though it varies.

### Is the recruiter trying to catch me out?

Usually not. They want a candidate who works out, and it's their job to check the basics.
