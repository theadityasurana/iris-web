It is a fair worry. You want help with the tedious part, but you don't want to look careless to the person reading your application.

## What a recruiter actually sees

A recruiter usually reviews a record in an applicant tracking system: your resume, your form answers, and any cover letter. Nothing on that record generally says who typed it. A careful human and a good tool produce similar-looking data.

We can't promise what every employer's systems log, and any tool that claims to be "undetectable" is promising something it cannot verify. A better goal is an application you'd be happy to defend in an interview.

## What gives automation away

Recruiters tend to spot the content, not the method:

- A cover letter that could apply to any company, or that names the wrong one
- Long, polished answers that don't sound like the resume next to them
- Skills claimed in a form but absent everywhere else
- Applications to roles far outside your background
- Answers that contradict each other across questions

## How to stay on the right side of this

1. **Check your profile data.** A tool repeats whatever you give it, including mistakes.
2. **Keep tailoring honest.** Emphasising relevant experience is fine. Claiming experience you don't have will surface in the interview. See [how to tailor a resume without lying](/blog/tailor-resume-without-lying/).
3. **Choose the jobs yourself where you can.** You will recognise the role when someone calls.
4. **Answer sensitive questions truthfully.** Work authorization and similar answers carry real consequences.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is using an auto-apply tool dishonest?

Getting help filling in a form isn't dishonest, as long as what's submitted is true. People have long used autofill and asked friends to review applications. The line is false information.

### Do employers reject applications because AI helped?

Some employers have policies on AI use in applications, so read the instructions on roles you care about. Most rejections still come down to fit and quality.
