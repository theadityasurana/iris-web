A thank-you email won't win you a job you weren't going to get. But it's a small courtesy, and it's a chance to add something you forgot to say.

## When and to whom

Send it within about a day, to each person you spoke with, if you have their address. Otherwise ask the recruiter to pass it along. Make each note a bit different, in case they compare.

## A template

> Hi Priya,
>
> Thanks for your time today. I enjoyed hearing about how your team is reworking the ledger, especially the question of how to migrate without downtime.
>
> It made me more interested in the role. I'd be glad to share more about the migration I led at Northwind if that's helpful.
>
> Best,
> Asha

## Adding something you forgot

If you fumbled an answer, a line can help: "I've been thinking about your question on caching, and I'd add that..." Keep it short.

## When you haven't heard back

If they gave a date, wait until it passes, plus a day or two. Then:

> Hi Priya, I hope you're well. You mentioned that you'd be making a decision around this week, so I wanted to check where things stand. I'm still very interested, and I'm happy to share anything else you need.

Send one or two follow-ups at most. See also [following up after applying](/blog/follow-up-after-applying-for-a-job/).

## What to avoid

- Long essays
- Pressure, such as "I have other offers" unless it's true and relevant
- Copying the same note to everyone
- Spelling a name wrong, so check before sending

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a thank-you email really matter?

Views differ among hiring managers. It costs little, and it's rarely a negative when it's brief.

### Can I send it on LinkedIn?

Email is more usual, but a LinkedIn message is fine if it's all you have.
