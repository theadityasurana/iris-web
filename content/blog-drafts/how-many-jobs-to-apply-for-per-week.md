Most advice on this question gives you a single number. A single number can't be right, because a new graduate applying to entry-level software roles and a finance director applying to a handful of senior openings are playing different games.

## Start with a sustainable target

A sensible starting point for most people is 10 to 20 well-matched applications a week. It is low enough that you can read each job description, and high enough to give you real data within a few weeks. Treat it as a starting guess you will adjust, not a rule.

Adjust it for your situation:

- **Early career, broad market:** more roles fit you, so aim higher.
- **Senior or specialist:** fewer roles fit, so apply to almost all of the good ones and spend the spare time on referrals.
- **Currently employed:** pick a number you can keep up for months. A smaller steady number beats a burst followed by nothing.

## Use replies as your signal

The number that tells you the most is your reply rate: human replies divided by applications sent. Check it after about 30 applications.

- **No replies at all:** more volume will not help. Your resume or your role targeting needs work. Our guide to [tailoring a resume honestly](/blog/tailor-resume-without-lying/) is a good next step.
- **Replies, but no interviews after screening:** look at how you describe your experience on the phone, and at whether your salary expectations are out of range.
- **Interviews, but no offers:** applications are not the problem. Practise interviewing.

## Protect your time

Volume only works if applying is cheap. If each application takes you 20 minutes, 20 a week is a part-time job. This is the problem [swipe-to-apply](/blog/swipe-to-apply-job-apps-explained/) tools try to solve: you still choose each job, but the form filling is handled for you. Iris does this by filling in the application on the company's own careers page after you swipe right.

Whatever tool you use, [track every application](/blog/how-to-track-job-applications/) so that your reply rate is a real number rather than a feeling.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is applying to 100 jobs a week too many?

It depends on whether they fit. A hundred roles you would really take is fine if you have a way to apply quickly. A hundred roles you never read will mostly waste your time and can annoy recruiters at companies you care about.

### How long should a job search take?

It varies widely with your field, level and location, so be wary of any precise figure. Plan your finances and energy for months rather than weeks, and review your approach every few weeks.
