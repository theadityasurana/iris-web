This opener seems easy and catches many people out. Treat it as your pitch for why this conversation makes sense.

## The structure

- **Present:** what you do now, with one thing you're proud of
- **Past:** the relevant path that got you here
- **Future:** why this role is the logical next step

Aim for about a minute.

## Example: experienced

"I'm a backend engineer at a payments startup, where I lead the team's reliability work. Before that, I spent three years at a bank building internal APIs, which is where I got interested in payments. I'm looking for a larger platform team, and your work on your ledger system is exactly what I'd like to do next."

## Example: fresher

"I just finished my computer science degree, and I spent my final year building a campus events app that students actually used. I also interned on a frontend team, where I fixed real bugs and shipped a small feature. I'd like to start as a frontend developer on a product team, and that's why this role stood out."

## Example: career changer

"I've spent six years teaching secondary school science, and for the past two I've led the design of our online curriculum. I found I enjoyed that most, so I finished a certificate in instructional design. Your role would let me work on that full time."

## What to leave out

Personal history, a walk through every job, salary needs, and anything negative about a past employer.

## Make it fit

Before each interview, change the "future" part, and pick a "present" highlight that's most relevant to the job. Reading the posting again takes two minutes, and Iris's interview prep for each job can help you see which questions are likely.

Then get ready for [behavioral questions](/blog/behavioral-interview-star-method/).

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should my answer be?

About a minute, maybe up to two for senior roles. If they want more, they'll ask.

### Should I memorize it?

Know the structure and key points, but don't recite it word for word. It will sound stiff.
