Keywords matter because recruiters search for them and because they signal relevance at a glance. The goal is not density. It's making sure that the right true words are there.

## Find them

Open five to ten postings for the job you want. Copy the requirements into one document and look for what repeats. You'll usually see three groups:

- **Hard skills and tools:** Python, Salesforce, financial modelling
- **Domain terms:** payments, B2B SaaS, clinical trials
- **Role language:** stakeholder management, roadmap, incident response

Repeated terms across postings tell you more than any single listing.

## Use them in context

A skills list is useful for scanning, but a term means more inside an achievement.

**Weak:** Skills: SQL, Python, dashboards.

**Stronger:** Built Python and SQL pipelines behind the weekly revenue dashboard used by the sales team.

The second one proves the skill and adds context.

## Match the wording

If the posting says "project management" and you wrote "managed projects," use their form where it's natural. For acronyms, include both once: "search engine optimization (SEO)."

## What to avoid

- Long blocks of keywords with no context
- Tools you've only seen, not used
- The same word in every bullet
- Hidden text, which we cover in [ATS resume myths](/blog/ats-resume-myths/)

## A quick check

Read your resume aloud. If a sentence sounds like a list of search terms, rewrite it.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many keywords should my resume have?

There's no target number. Cover the skills you truly have that keep appearing for your role, then stop.

### Should I change keywords for each application?

Small adjustments help for roles you care about. Your core terms will stay similar if you're targeting one kind of job.
