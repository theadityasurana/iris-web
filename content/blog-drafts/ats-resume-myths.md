Few parts of job searching attract more fear and sales copy than the ATS. Here's a calmer view.

## What an ATS is

An applicant tracking system is software that employers use to post jobs, collect applications, move candidates through stages and search their records. Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby and Workday are well-known examples. Its main users are recruiters.

## Myth: "Most resumes are rejected by a robot"

You'll see confident percentages online, usually with no solid source. We won't repeat them. Some automatic rejection does exist, typically from knockout questions like work authorization. Many resumes go unread simply because recruiters have too many, which is different from a robot judging you.

## Myth: "You need a 100% ATS score"

Resume scanners compare your text with a posting. They can reveal missing terms, which is useful, but the score isn't what employers see. Stuffing keywords to raise a number makes your resume worse for the human reading it.

## Myth: "Hide keywords in white text"

Don't. Parsed text can show this up in plain view, and it looks dishonest.

## What actually matters

- **Parsing:** use a simple layout so your details are extracted correctly. Complex tables, text boxes and headers can cause trouble in some systems.
- **Searchability:** recruiters search for skills and titles, so make sure the true, relevant terms appear.
- **Screening answers:** get these right. See [screening questions](/blog/job-application-screening-questions/).
- **Readability:** a person makes the real decision.

For the existing basics, read [how to write an ATS-friendly resume](/blog/ats-friendly-resume/). Iris also shows an ATS keyword check for each job, as a guide to what's missing, and not as a goal in itself.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use a two-column resume?

Many modern systems handle them, but results vary. A single column is the safest choice.

### Should my file be PDF or DOCX?

Follow the employer's instructions if they give any. Otherwise see [PDF vs DOCX](/blog/resume-pdf-or-docx/).
