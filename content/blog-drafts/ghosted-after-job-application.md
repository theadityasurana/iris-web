You sent a good application and heard nothing. It's frustrating, and it's also very common.

## Why it happens

From the outside, it can look like a judgment on you. Usually it's more mundane:

- **Too many applicants** for the team to answer each one
- **The role changed,** paused or got cancelled
- **An internal or referred candidate** was already well along
- **The recruiter moved on** to other priorities
- **The application never reached a person** because of a screening question

## What to do for one application

Wait about a week, then [send one short follow-up](/blog/follow-up-after-applying-for-a-job/) if you can find the right person. Then close it in your tracker and move on. Waiting on a single company is the costliest mistake here, so keep applying in parallel.

## What to do if it's every application

One silence means nothing. Thirty silences are a signal. Check these, in order:

1. **Targeting.** Are you applying at the right level? Compare your experience with the role's actual requirements.
2. **Resume.** Could a stranger tell in ten seconds what you do? See [how to write a resume summary](/blog/how-to-write-a-resume-summary/).
3. **Screening answers.** A wrong answer to a knockout question can end things automatically. See [screening questions](/blog/job-application-screening-questions/).
4. **Contact details.** Check that the phone number and email on your application are right, and look in your spam folder.
5. **Channel.** Try [referrals](/blog/get-a-job-referral-without-connections/). Warm introductions can change who looks at your application.

## Look after yourself too

A long search takes a toll. Set a fixed amount of time for it, and step away the rest of the day. Measure what you control, like applications sent and people contacted, instead of what you don't.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long before I should assume I've been rejected?

There's no fixed timeline. If it's been a few weeks and a follow-up got no response, treat it as closed. You can still be pleasantly surprised.

### Should I apply again to the same company?

Yes, for a different role that fits, or after your experience has changed.
