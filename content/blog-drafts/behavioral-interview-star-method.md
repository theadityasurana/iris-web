Behavioral questions start with "Tell me about a time when..." They assume your past behaviour tells them something about the future. STAR gives you a way to answer clearly.

## The four parts

- **Situation:** the context, in a sentence or two
- **Task:** what you needed to accomplish
- **Action:** what you did, with "I" rather than "we"
- **Result:** what changed, with numbers if you have them, and what you learned

Most of your time should go on the action.

## A worked example

**Question:** Tell me about a time you handled a difficult stakeholder.

**Situation:** "A sales lead kept asking for changes in the final week of our release."

**Task:** "I had to protect the launch date without losing their trust."

**Action:** "I sat down with them, and I listed each request with what it would cost. We agreed on two that truly affected deals, and we moved the rest into the next release, with dates."

**Result:** "We launched on time, and they became one of our strongest supporters. After that, we set a weekly check-in so that surprises came up earlier."

## Build a story bank

Prepare five or six true stories, each of which can answer several questions:

- A project you're proud of
- A conflict or disagreement
- A failure or mistake
- A tight deadline
- Leading without authority
- Learning something quickly

## Common mistakes

- Spending two minutes on background
- Saying "we" so much that your part disappears
- Having no result
- Choosing a fake failure like "I work too hard"

## Practise aloud

Time yourself, and aim for about two minutes per story. Iris's interview prep gives you likely questions with STAR-style answers for specific jobs, which you can adapt with your own true examples.

## Frequently asked questions

### What if I have no work experience?

Use projects, classes, internships or volunteering. Interviewers care about how you think and act.

### What if I don't have a result yet?

Share what you'd measured so far and what you learned. Honest and partial is better than made up.
