If you've applied through Workday, you know the routine: a new account for every employer, a verification email, and a password you'll never remember. Here is why, and how to handle it safely.

## Why this happens

Companies buy applicant tracking systems, and some of them set up separate candidate accounts per employer. The account lets you return, check your status and edit your details. So your account at one company often doesn't carry over to the next.

## The security rule that matters

Never reuse the password from your personal email, your bank or your employer. If one job site is breached, a reused password can expose those accounts too. Since job sites hold your personal data, they deserve some care.

## Two sensible options

**A password manager** generates and stores a unique password per site. This is the best option for most people.

**A dedicated application password** is a single strong password used only for job sites, and nowhere else. It isn't as strong as unique passwords, but because it's separate from your important accounts, the damage from a leak is limited. Pair it with a dedicated email address, so verification messages don't drown your personal inbox.

## What makes a strong one

Use at least 12 characters with upper and lower case letters, numbers and symbols. A random generator is better than anything you think up yourself.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I delete old job site accounts?

Often, but the process depends on the employer's system. Look for a privacy or account settings option, or contact the employer.

### Why do I need a verification code?

Many systems confirm your email address before you can apply. Keep that inbox handy, as codes can expire quickly.
