This question feels like a trap because the first number can anchor the talk. Preparation takes most of the pressure off.

## Do your research

Look at salary ranges in postings for similar jobs in your location, salary sites, and what people in your network will share. Consider total compensation: base, bonus, equity and benefits. In India, make clear whether you're talking about fixed pay or total CTC.

## What to say early on

You can ask first: "Could you share the budgeted range for this role? I'd like to make sure we're aligned."

If they insist: "Based on my research and experience, I'm looking at a range of X to Y, depending on the whole package and the role's scope."

Make X a figure that you'd truly accept. People often hear the bottom of your range.

## On application forms

If the field is required, enter a researched figure. If a form forces a single number, choose one you'd be happy with. Keep your answers consistent across applications, since [screening answers](/blog/job-application-screening-questions/) may be stored.

## If they ask your current salary

Laws differ. Some places restrict employers from asking about your pay history. Where it's allowed and you'd rather not say: "I'd prefer to focus on the value of this role and the range you've budgeted." Where it's normal to share, as in many Indian hiring processes, be truthful, since it may be verified.

## Later, at offer stage

An offer is when you have the most leverage. Read [how to negotiate a job offer](/blog/how-to-negotiate-a-job-offer/) before you respond.

Iris keeps your current and expected compensation in your profile, for forms that require them. Revisit them as your search goes on.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it bad to give a range?

No. It's standard, provided that you'd accept the low end.

### What if their budget is below my range?

Ask whether there's flexibility, or whether other parts of the package can move. Decide beforehand what your real minimum is.
